Research Overview
Understanding materials through microstructure, physics-based modeling, and data-driven design.

The M2D2 Lab studies how microstructures form, evolve, and control material properties. We combine multiphysics modeling, mesoscale simulation, machine learning, and interpretable data-driven analysis to reveal processing–microstructure–property relationships in energy materials, structural alloys, and architected porous systems. Our goal is to convert complex microstructural information into mechanistic understanding and actionable design principles for materials operating in energy and extreme environments.

Microstructure Informatics and AI-Guided Materials Design

Microstructures contain rich information that governs transport, mechanics, and degradation, but extracting physically meaningful design rules from high-dimensional data remains challenging. We develop microstructure informatics methods that integrate simulation-generated datasets, image-based learning, physically motivated descriptors, interpretable machine learning, causal analysis, and regime discovery to connect morphology with effective properties.

Keywords: microstructure–property relationships; interpretable AI; causal analysis; representation learning; porous microstructures; solid-state battery composites.
Representative publications:
  • Machine learning insights into microstructural origins of transport and mechanical properties in porous microstructures
  • Machine-learning-assisted deciphering of microstructural effects on ionic transport in composite materials: A case study of Li7La3Zr2O12–LiCoO2
  • Integrated framework to model microstructure evolution and decipher the microstructure–property relationship in polymeric porous materials

Chemo-Mechanical Coupling and Degradation in Energy Materials

Energy materials operate under coupled chemical, electrochemical, and mechanical driving forces. We develop computational models to understand how ion transport, interfacial reactions, phase transformations, stress evolution, cracking, and microstructural heterogeneity interact during battery operation. This work emphasizes solid-state batteries, cathode and solid-electrolyte materials, grain-boundary transport, and degradation mechanisms emerging from coupled fields.

Keywords: solid-state batteries; ion transport; chemo-mechanics; phase transformation; cracking; grain boundaries; degradation modeling.
Representative publications:
  • Computational simulation of asymmetric phase transformation in cracked Li7La3Zr2O12: Variant selection and chemo-mechanical implications
  • Modeling single-crystal battery materials: From fundamental understanding to performance evaluation
  • Machine-learning-assisted deciphering of microstructural effects on ionic transport in composite materials

Defect-Mediated Phase Transformations and Alloy Design

Defects are not only damage sources; they can also provide local pathways for phase transformation, solute redistribution, and mechanical response. We study how dislocations, stacking faults, grain boundaries, precipitates, and applied stresses mediate phase transformations and deformation mechanisms in structural alloys. By combining phase-field modeling, thermodynamic analysis, and mechanistic simulations, we aim to identify defect-level design principles for improved strength, creep resistance, transformation stability, and functional performance.

Keywords: superalloys; dislocations; stacking faults; precipitates; phase-field modeling; localized phase transformation; alloy design.
Representative publications:
  • Localized phase transformation at stacking faults and mechanism-based alloy design
  • Generalized stacking fault energy surface mismatch and dislocation transformation
  • Phase field modeling of shearing processes of a dual-lobed γ″|γ′|γ″ coprecipitate
  • Stress-dependent χ phase transformation in a Ni-based superalloy

Architected Porous and Interpenetrating Materials for Energy Applications

Architected porous and interpenetrating microstructures provide powerful routes to control transport, reaction, and mechanical response. We investigate how connectivity, tortuosity, phase distribution, and 3D architecture regulate ion diffusion, fluid transport, gas evolution, and structural integrity in energy materials. This research spans interpenetrating electrodes, nanoporous separators, polymer-derived porous materials, hydrogen-storage structures, and multifunctional architected materials.

Keywords: architected materials; porous media; interpenetrating electrodes; transport; water splitting; hydrogen storage; polymerization-induced phase separation.
Representative publications:
  • Random 3D interpenetrating electrode design for energy storage applications
  • Interpenetrated structures for enhancing ion diffusion kinetics in electrochemical energy storage devices
  • Interpenetrating 3D electrodes for high-rate alkaline water splitting
  • Nanocone-modified surface facilitates gas bubble detachment for high-rate alkaline water splitting
  • Tailored additive design of scaffold-free porous Mg for ultimate hydrogen storage
